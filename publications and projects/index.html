TumorPINN Lab: Estimating Tumour-Growth Parameters with Spectral Solvers, CNNs and Physics-Informed Neural Networks

In this project I look at how a tumour spreads and grows, using the Fisher–KPP reaction–diffusion model. From noisy measurements of tumour density, the goal is to recover the two quantities that drive the model: how fast cells diffuse and how fast they proliferate. I compare four ways of doing this on the same data: a convolutional network, a classical spectral least-squares fit, a PINN, and a PINN started from the network's estimate. The same idea is then applied to real growth curves from public mouse-tumour datasets, with thousands of PINNs trained together in GPU batches. Everything is collected in an interactive dashboard.

A Numerical Method for a Zero-Dimensional Lumped-Parameter Blood Flow Model

Here I model blood flow with vessel compartments connected to Windkessel terminal models, which gives a system of ODEs for pressure, flow and volume. I solve it with the forward Euler method and calibrate the parameters against synthetic data using optimization.
